What it is

Quiet, continuous attention

The sensor is about the size of a small book. It mounts on the wall of the room where you sleep and needs a power outlet nearby. You never touch it.

Nothing to wear

No wristband, no clip, no charging, nothing to remember at bedtime. The sensor works across the room while you sleep normally.

No cameras or microphones

It uses radar, not pictures or sound. It measures heart rate, breathing, and movement — it cannot see or hear you.

Real clinicians reading it

Board-certified physicians and licensed clinicians review the data and pass what matters to the people caring for you.

How it works

Four steps, and then it is quiet

  1. You answer a few questions. About three minutes, on this website.
  2. Our clinical team calls you. They confirm what Medicare requires and answer your questions.
  3. We set up the sensor. It goes on the bedroom wall near an outlet, with a clear view of the bed.
  4. You go back to normal. The monitoring runs on its own, every night.

Why it matters

Changes often show up at night, first

Breathing, heart rate, and how much someone moves in bed tend to shift before a person feels different during the day. Watching those patterns every night gives a care team a chance to look into a change sooner.

Researchers studying contactless in-home monitoring in older adults have found that night-to-night patterns can act as an early signal of a health change.1 Studies of remote patient monitoring programs for older adults with several chronic conditions have reported fewer unplanned hospital admissions and emergency visits.23

These are published findings from other programs and technologies, not a promise about your own care. Whether monitoring helps you, and whether Medicare covers it, depends on your health and your doctor's judgment.
